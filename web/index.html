Four facets

One system, four pieces, all on Monad.

Each piece runs on its own. Together they make compute something an agent can price, pay for, hold and redeem without an account on either side.

[ 01 ]

Pay for prices

cloud.trycorpus.ai serves spot, on-demand, reserved and GPU prices across providers, 29 priced endpoints and 35 MCP tools. Every 402 carries a USDC offer on Monad, settled by the Monad facilitator with no API key on either side. An agent pays a cent, gets the price back signed.

How x402 works on the feed
[ 02 ]

Verify them on chain

Each day the feed publishes index fixings, EIP-712 signed by the corpusAI publisher key and attested on chain. FixingOracle on Monad checks that signature, so anyone can relay a fixing and nobody can forge one. Any contract can read the H100 price.

Governance and the signing scheme
[ 03 ]

Mint compute

ComputeToken is an ERC-1155 where one token is one permanently funded corpus machine, priced by corpus's own underwriting: principal plus buffer, so the yield covers the bill. Minting is the payment in USDC; the buyer holds a transferable claim. Redeeming burns it and tells corpus which account gets the position.

Mint a machine
[ 04 ]

Put the float to work

Mint payments land in the treasury and are swept into a curated USDC lending vault on Morpho, with Aave v3 as fallback, instead of sitting idle. The yield subsidises the compute. Withdraw when the hosting bills fall due.

How it connects

From a price to a machine.

The same USDC on Monad moves through every step. The oracle is the public reference for what compute costs; the token is the claim on it; the treasury is where the money waits.

priceAn agent asks the feed for a price and pays the 402 in USDC on Monad. The answer carries the publisher's signature.GET /v1/index/fixing → 402 → eip155:143
verifyAnyone posts the signed fixing to FixingOracle. The contract recovers the signer and stores the value under its day.oracle.post(fixing, sig) · latest("CX.H100SXM.NEO")
mintA buyer picks a VM size and pays principal plus buffer, once. The token is minted to their wallet; the USDC goes to the treasury.token.mint(id, qty) → Minted
redeemThe holder burns the token naming a corpus account. corpus opens the position and the principal's yield pays the machine's bill from then on.token.redeem(id, qty, accountRef) → Redeemed

Why Monad: 400 ms blocks and sub-second finality make per-call settlement feel like an ordinary API round trip, and the USDC agents pay with is the same asset Morpho, Aave and Euler lend on Monad, so the float never has to leave the chain.

Disclosures

What was built when, and what is not built yet.

Listed so nobody mistakes the demo for the product.

Built in the window
The Monad x402 rail on the feed (1 Oct), EIP-712 fixing signatures and daily attestations (27 Sep), the hosted MCP server, FixingOracle, ComputeToken, the mint page, the clients and the treasury sweep (10 to 13 Oct 2026).
Pre-existing foundation
The corpusAI Cloud Pricing feed (collectors, archive, index methodology, API) and the corpus compute platform on Ethereum with its deposit flow predate the hackathon. Everything here consumes the feed as an external service and points the existing deposit flow at Monad.
Not yet built
The corpus-api side of redemption: the wallet-to-account link, a VM-terms ledger beside the USD ledger, and the watcher that indexes Redeemed and provisions the machine. Also secondary liquidity, price hedging for GPU sizes, and the legal treatment of a tradeable claim on a service.
Why Fly-sized VMs first
Their bill is a catalog number set by corpus, and the position price follows from corpus's underwriting (k = 1.15, r = 7.24%), so none of the GPU forward dynamics apply yet. The oracle is the public price reference for the GPU sizes that come next.
Team and tools
Julian Traversa and Alexandra, one team, one submission. Developed with AI assistance (Claude Code) under the team's direction; every design decision, key and deployment is the team's.
