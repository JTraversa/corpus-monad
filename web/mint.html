Mint compute

A machine,
as a token.

One token is one permanently funded corpus machine: a position whose principal is never spent and whose yield pays the monthly bill for as long as it is open. While you hold it, jobs routed to the idle machine earn you yield. It trades on its own value. And it redeems, any time, for the machine itself. The price is corpus's own underwriting, principal plus a buffer, paid once in USDC on Monad.

Not connected Monad Testnet · chain 10143

Sizes

Fly-modeled machine sizes, priced as permanent positions: price = k · C / r with the monthly bill C, yield rate r of 7.24% and buffer k of 1.15, exactly as corpus quotes them. Outstanding is the number of machines minted and not yet redeemed.

Connect a wallet on Monad to mint. Nothing here moves without your signature.
[ 01 ]

Pay once

The mint is the payment. Principal plus buffer in USDC goes straight to the corpus treasury on Monad, where it is swept into the lending vault and starts earning.

[ 02 ]

Hold, and earn

The token is an ordinary ERC-1155 backed by a principal that is never spent. It can be sent or sold, and while it is unredeemed, jobs routed to the idle machine pay the holder. The job router is the next build; GPU sizes come first.

[ 03 ]

Redeem when ready

Burn it naming a corpus account. The Redeemed event is the instruction to open the position; corpus indexes it the way it indexes deposits, and the yield pays the bill from then on.

[ 04 ]

Not yet wired

The job router and the corpus-api side of redemption are the next steps after the hackathon. Today a redeem is a real burn and a real event; the position is opened by hand, and no job yield accrues yet.
